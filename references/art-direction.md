# Photo Abstract Editorial Art Direction

- Source analysis

## Source analysis

Treat the supplied photograph as the sole factual source. Before generating a motif, identify three to six decisive facts from:

- relative scale and left/right or upper/lower position;
- horizontal/vertical axes, curves, movement, and pauses;
- intervals, repetition, overlap, depth, and asymmetry;
- light/dark hierarchy, color roles, and negative space.

Retain relationships, not surface detail. The motif should read first as a sparse abstract composition and only later recall the particular photograph.

## Abstraction system

Choose one primary mark family and at most two supporting families.

Primary families:

- flat or slightly organic color masses;
- soft irregular circles;
- arcing or tapered strokes;
- short bars or stacked bands;
- simplified architectural masses.

Supporting families:

- fine structural axes;
- one primary and one subordinate vertical anchor when the source contains decisive vertical structure;
- one or two narrow negative-space cuts or slight horizontal band offsets;
- restrained human ink marks;
- a small amount of irregular repetition.

Map subjects conservatively:

- Landscapes, water, horizons, and roads: use two to four irregular bands and one dark structural line. If a pole, mast, or tower is compositionally decisive, translate it into exactly two unequal irregular vertical anchors: one taller and slightly thicker, one shorter and thinner. Keep both subordinate to the horizontal bands and omit crossarms, wires, latticework, and complete infrastructure outlines. Otherwise use one or two negative gaps or band offsets.
- Landmark architecture: retain one to three identity cues such as a taper, arch, spire, eave line, or characteristic negative space; omit windows and surface ornament.
- Organic groups such as balloons, trees, clouds, or lights: use overlapping soft masses without realistic highlights or internal patterns.
- People: use one irregular continuous short vertical mark per person; do not draw heads, faces, limbs, or clothing.
- Small representative objects: retain two or three planar cues based on scale and position; omit realistic volume and material.

Every important mark must answer a visible fact. Avoid regularized spacing, invented symmetry, generic icons, literal tracing, complete illustration, and decorative filler.

## Adaptive composition

Keep the full photograph and join it directly to a flat lower panel. Do not use frames, tape, shadows, torn edges, mockups, or collage effects.

The composer selects these default panel heights relative to the rendered photograph:

| Source orientation | Panel height |
|---|---:|
| Landscape, aspect at least 1.2 | 80% of photo height |
| Near square | 70% of photo height |
| Portrait, aspect at most 0.8 | 55% of photo height |

Use one editorial grid without forcing every element onto an identical numeric margin. For lower-left layouts, place the motif at a 7% structural inset and shift the title optically inward by about 1.2% of canvas width. Place the motif 16% down from the panel top at about 54% of canvas width; restrained variants may use 48–58%. Give the title a deeper bottom inset, around 11.5% of canvas width. Keep the motif below 38% of panel height and preserve internal intervals, overlap, center of gravity, and asymmetry. Remove isolated alpha components and chroma-key specks before compositing.

Use a uniform warm ivory panel by default (`#E8E1D5`). The panel itself must contain no gradient, lighting variation, shadow, glow, vignette, seam, grain, paper texture, watercolor wash, noise, stain, haze, or scan artifact.

## Color and typography

Extract colors only from the photograph and reduce saturation and quantity. Use one dominant role, one dark structural role, one light/neutral role, and at most one or two small accents. Prefer oxidized, mineral, or ink-like versions of source colors; never add unsupported neon or complementary colors.

Create one original English title, preferably two to five words, grounded in visible subject, light, time, motion, or spatial relationships. Avoid travel advertising, literal place descriptions, photography jargon, generic `Memory`, `Dream`, or `Moment`, and unrelated grand narratives.

Add a three-to-seven-word subtitle only when it contributes a new idea. Render title strings locally and exactly. Prefer Bodoni or another high-contrast editorial serif, with Baskerville or Garamond as fallbacks. Use the font's native whole-run kerning rather than uniform character spacing. One short connector may be 65–75% of the primary size in the matching italic face; do not stylize multiple words. Use a photo-derived dark color and choose lower-left or bottom-center alignment. Judge margins optically, not by forced equality, and keep all text inside the panel away from the motif and canvas edge.

## Acceptance criteria

The final work contains exactly:

1. one deterministic rendering of the full source photograph;
2. one uniform panel;
3. one traceable abstract motif;
4. one exact English title;
5. at most one exact optional subtitle.

Reject any result containing extra words, letters inside the motif, numbers, dates, place labels, color swatches, legends, signatures, logos, watermarks, generated photo content, aggressive crop, filters, outpainting, vector tracing, recognizable traced objects, isolated chroma specks, dense decoration, or non-uniform panel texture.
