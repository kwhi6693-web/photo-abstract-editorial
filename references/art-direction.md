# Photo Abstract Editorial Art Direction

This reference defines the domain language shared by all execution modes. The supplied photograph is the sole factual source. Preserve relationships over surface detail and make every important mark traceable to a visible fact.

## Core principles

- Identify three to six decisive facts: scale, position, axes, movement, intervals, overlap, depth, asymmetry, light/dark hierarchy, color roles, and negative space.
- The motif should read as sparse abstraction before it recalls the source. Use one primary mark family and at most two supporting families.
- Prefer flat or organic color masses, soft irregular circles, tapered strokes, short bars/bands, simplified architectural masses, structural axes, restrained rhythm, and negative-space cuts.
- Preserve source-derived direction, scale, rhythm, center of gravity, and asymmetry. Do not invent symmetry, generic icons, surface ornament, or literal tracing.
- Use a photo-derived muted palette: one dominant role, one dark structural role, one light/neutral role, and at most one or two small accents.
- Keep a full readable photograph joined directly to a uniform warm-ivory panel. No frame, tape, shadow, torn edge, collage effect, gradient, texture, grain, or seam.
- Use generous whitespace and a restrained editorial hierarchy. The panel contains one exact English title and only an optional exact subtitle when it adds a distinct idea.
- Render typography locally and exactly when Strict Mode is available. Use native whole-run kerning, a high-contrast serif, and at most one smaller italic connector.

## Hard prohibitions

No invented people, buildings, objects, geography, text, logos, watermarks, swatches, legends, dates, place labels, unsupported decoration, aggressive crop, outpainting, complete illustration, or recognizable vector tracing.

Decisive vertical structures may become one primary and one subordinate vertical anchor, unequal in height and width, only when the source supports them. They remain subordinate to the main rhythm and omit crossarms, wires, latticework, and complete infrastructure outlines.

The final work is accepted only when the source facts, abstraction level, spatial relationships, typography, panel, and editorial coherence pass the structured quality check. See the modular Scene, Control, Layout, Quality, and Series references for their specialized rules.
